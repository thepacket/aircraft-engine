What is and is not validated

The Model tab opens with the list of open items: known gaps between this model and the real engines that remain unfixed (the V2527-A5 fuel flow and acceleration, the PT6A-42 propeller overspeed that withholds its reverse lesson, the prescribed autorotation, the unmeasured start transients). The Spec tab repeats the one that applies to the selected engine. Treat numbers from an affected preset as illustrative.

Five engine families

Pick the engine in the top bar. Turbofan (CFM56-7B26, LEAP-1B28 of the 737 MAX, V2527-A5 of the A320, or the PW1127G-JM geared turbofan of the A320neo): starter, fuel lever at 25% N2, idle at N1 21% (LEAP: 19%, V2500: 20%). Piston (O-360, or the O-320 of the Cessna 172): mixture rich, 2-3 primer shots cold, magnetos BOTH, crank; mag check at 1,800 rpm. Turboprop (PT6A-114A, or the PT6A-42 of the King Air B200): prop lever full, starter, condition lever to LOW IDLE at 12-15% Ng, starter off at idle. Turbojet (J85-GE-5A of the T-38): starter, throttle out of CUTOFF at ~14% rpm, idle at 48%; the throttle runs idle → MIL → MAX A/B (1.0 → 1.3), the nozzle closes toward MIL and opens when the afterburner lights. Turboshaft (250-C20B of the JetRanger): starter, throttle twist to IDLE at 15% N1, starter off at 58%, then throttle FULL and the N2 governor holds the rotor at 100% Nr while the collective sets the load.

Scenario script

at 0     starter on
at 18    fuel on
when n2 >= 59   note idle reached
at 70    tla 1.0           # 0..1
when n1 >= 95   fault surge
at 100   tla 0
at 120   engine 2          # or 1, all
at 120   fault ignition_fail on
at 18    watch egt < 725    # must hold to the end
at 90    check n1 >= 20     # evaluated when the line fires
at 200   end

Timed lines: at <s> <cmd>. Conditional lines: when <signal> <op> <value> <cmd>, armed at the preceding at time, fire once. Signals: n1, n2, egt, ff, thrust, oilp, oilt, vib, t4, sm_hpc, alt, mach… (any chart channel).

Commands: engine 1|2|all, ctl <control> <value> (any control of the engine: tla, mixture, magnetos, power_lever, prop_lever, condition_lever, carb_heat…), starter, fuel on|off (fuel lever / mixture / condition lever), tla x (main lever), n1 pct|off, alt m, mach x, isa dK, humidity x, running level, antiice, pack, reverser, fault name [value], bottle, log on|off, speed x, note text, screenshot [label] (saves a PNG of the flight deck), check <signal> <op> <value>, watch <signal> <op> <value>, end. Checkpoints are graded: green when passed, red when failed, with the measured value in the status line. Every graded run is kept in the Results tab (score, per-check details, CSV / JSON export). Lessons written for a specific engine preset (starts, takeoffs, hot starts, a surge, a flameout) are listed first in the lesson dropdown.

Parametric sweep

The sweep display unit plots any signal against altitude, Mach, ΔISA or lever position at steady state, with up to six values of a second variable as separate curves. Points are computed on a settled copy of engine 1; the variables not swept stay at the current environment and lever. CSV exports the curves.

Layout

Drag the grip left of the strip charts to resize the charts column, and the grip under the engine indications to resize them: the dials rearrange to make the most of the space. Double-click a grip to go back to the automatic layout. Both positions are remembered in this browser. While a scenario runs, the editor shows the current line in white and armed when lines in amber.

Replay

Load a CSV flight log, map its columns, and the simulator is driven by the logged levers (or follows the logged N1). Logged channels are overlaid on the charts as dashed lines and the errors are tabulated.

Use

Educational tool only. Not a certified training device and not a substitute for the aircraft and engine manuals. MIT licensed; source and model documentation on GitHub.

Offline and install

After the first visit the simulator works offline: a service worker keeps the app, the wasm core and the lessons. The browser's install option (address bar or menu) adds it as an app on a desktop or tablet.

Sharing

Share copies a link that puts the scenario, a modified spec and the settings into the URL fragment: nothing is sent to the server. Scenario and spec edits are also kept in this browser. Screenshot saves a PNG of the whole flight deck, including the part of the centre column scrolled out of view, named after the engine and the simulation time.

Parametric sweep (steady state; variables not swept stay at the current settings)

What it does

Computes the engine's steady-state performance at a series of operating points and plots one signal against one variable, without flying the simulation through each point. Each point is a copy of engine 1, reset, placed running and settled for 40 s; the live engines are untouched and any injected faults are cleared on the copy. Points are equilibrium values, so transients such as EGT overshoot during an acceleration do not appear.

How to run one

  1. Set what you are not sweeping. The sweep holds every other variable at the current environment inputs (altitude, Mach, ΔISA, humidity) and the current main lever. After a reset the lever is at idle, so a thrust sweep would show idle thrust: set the lever (or use N1 / lever as X or series) first.
  2. X: altitude, Mach, ΔISA, lever position, or N1 (turbofans). The range fills with a default you can edit; steps is the number of points (2–60).
  3. Y: any signal of the engine, the same list as the strip charts (thrust, TSFC, EGT, fuel flow, surge margin…). Units follow the SI / Imperial selector.
  4. Series (optional): a second variable with up to six comma-separated values; each value becomes a curve. On the turbofans, choosing N1 as X or series holds N1 instead of the lever.
  5. Run sweep, then CSV to export the curves.

Studies worth doing

The buttons below fill the fields in; set the lever or environment first where the study needs it.

  • Thrust lapse: net thrust vs altitude for several Mach numbers. Why climb thrust falls off, and how ram effect helps at speed.
  • TSFC vs Mach at several N1: the classic turbofan curves and the reason cruise is flown at high Mach and high N1.
  • Hot-day flat rating: thrust or N1 vs ΔISA with the lever at TO/GA. The curve kinks at the flat-rating corner where the EGT limit takes over.
  • Engine comparison: run the same sweep on the CFM56, LEAP-1B, V2500 and PW1100G and compare the CSVs; the cruise TSFC gap between generations stands out.
  • Piston altitude lapse: brake power or manifold pressure vs altitude at full throttle, the loss of a normally aspirated engine.
  • Turboprop limits: torque, then ITT, vs ΔISA at max power lever: where the engine goes from torque-limited to temperature-limited.
  • Surge margin: HPC surge margin vs N1 for several altitudes, before writing a surge lesson.
